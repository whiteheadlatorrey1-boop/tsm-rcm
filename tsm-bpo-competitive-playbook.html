01 · Battlecards
Competitor Category Battlecards

Four categories a BPO staffing sale most commonly runs into. Each card: who they are, where they win, where they're weak, and the one-line reframe to use live.

National Staffing Vendors
Scale Play
Where they winBrand recognition, national account contracts, bench depth across many metros.
Where they're weakGeneric account coverage, slow escalation paths, junior reps rotate off accounts, no local Phoenix-market intelligence.
Reframe"You get national scale with a call center behind it. We give you the same coverage with a named team that knows this market and answers the phone."
Regional Temp Agencies
Local Play
Where they winExisting local relationships, lower per-placement fees, fast informal turnaround for common roles.
Where they're weakManual matching, no compliance/reporting layer, thin bench for specialized or urgent roles, no decision-intelligence tooling.
Reframe"Same local responsiveness you already like — plus the reporting and compliance trail your finance and legal teams are going to start asking for."
In-House Recruiting Build
DIY Play
Where they winFull control, no vendor markup, deep internal culture fit knowledge.
Where they're weakFixed headcount cost regardless of hiring volume, slow to scale up or down, no market-rate/compensation benchmarking, single points of failure.
Reframe"Keep the control. Outsource the volatility — we flex up for a hiring surge and flex down without you carrying idle recruiter headcount."
Freelance / Gig Platforms
Marketplace Play
Where they winFast, cheap for one-off or short-term roles, self-service, no long sales cycle.
Where they're weakNo vetting depth for regulated or client-facing roles, no ongoing account management, quality is inconsistent at volume.
Reframe"Great for a single seat. We're built for when you need twenty seats filled correctly, on a deadline, with someone accountable for quality."
02 · Win / Loss Analysis
Deal Pattern Framework

Recurring patterns to log after every closed-won and closed-lost deal. The value is in the pattern, not any single deal — review this quarterly, not per-deal.

Patterns behind wins
  • Buyer had a named, painful hiring surge (seasonal, contract win, expansion) with a hard deadline
  • We got in front of the decision-maker directly instead of a gatekeeper
  • We showed a live tool (dashboard, pipeline view) instead of a slide deck
  • Reference customer in the same vertical was available for a call
  • Pricing was framed against cost-of-vacancy, not against competitor rate card
Patterns behind losses
  • Incumbent vendor relationship was longer than our entire sales cycle
  • Buyer never got past procurement/legal — deal died in paperwork, not in the pitch
  • We led with price instead of speed-to-fill or quality of match
  • No internal champion once our main contact changed roles or left
  • Couldn't produce a reference in the buyer's specific sub-vertical

04 · Market Focus
Phoenix + National Positioning

Phoenix Metro

Lead with local responsiveness and same-day escalation — the thing out-of-state national vendors structurally can't match. Name-drop the metro's actual growth sectors (logistics/distribution, healthcare, semiconductor-adjacent manufacturing) when relevant to the buyer's industry, and confirm current figures before quoting them in a live pitch since local labor-market conditions shift quickly.

National Accounts

Lead with the decision-intelligence layer (BNCA-style dashboards, exposure tracking, explainability) as the differentiator against commodity national vendors — multi-location buyers care more about consistent reporting across sites than about any single site's turnaround time.
